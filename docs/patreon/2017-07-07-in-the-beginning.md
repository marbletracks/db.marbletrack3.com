The animation of the construction of Marble Track 3 opens with fourteen frames, a bit over a second of video taken of blue fabric on my desk.

It had taken a while to get there. I tried to start this in 2011.  I built a tripod for my waterproof camera, waited for the glue to dry, placed down a piece of wood and shot some frames.

There was apparently a second attmept but I have no biological memory of it, just this vidoe labeled "Third attempt" (May 2013) https://youtu.be/kSdHNDlQ1x8 I still only made a minute and 9 seconds of video, but it was a great start!

On one of the above attempts, I had to give up because the frames of video were inaccessible after I encrypted the backup and subsequently lost the password.  I was so afraid someone would steal the hard work!  I was sure I was smart enough to keep a password safe.

The first three versions of Marble Track 3 were too difficult to work with because it was fixed on the table and I couldn't easily reach to the back without knocking something over.

Finally, in summer of 2017, after Lin and I had bought a house, and no plans to move!  I decided to start the project again.

I had one clear idea: the stage needed to rotate.  How?  I went to a home center and looked at what they had available.  I found round wooden platters and a lazy susan type bearing.  This should work.  I bought two wooden platters knowing I would need them, and a third one just in case.

The wooden platters were *far* too big for the characters to move.  Rabby had moved the stage into place in the third attempt.  Now Francois would be able to move the second stage into place.

Even the bearing was too heavy for the characters but I didn't have anyone else who could move it..
My friend Mayumi had given me a skeleton toy made out of plastic that I had to put together so I decided he could be the radio operator to bring in the bearing.

84 seconds of video, just me making the skeleton!

Now that we had a house, I was more confident I could settle in for a big project.  Soon enough I had blown past however many frames I had lost before.

Meanwhile, in the story: this is a plot of land where a park is going to be built.
The crew hasn't arrived. The residents haven't moved in. Squarehead is about to walk
in backwards, leading Rabby, who is dragging the base behind him.  Six days later he walked off set,
crushing Squarehead in the process.  Why?  No one knows but I thought it was funny to have a big animal innocently crush the guy, even funnier that he could just get up again and be okay.

Rabby was very floppy and hard to work with and was so big he was headed right to the camera.  Should he look inside?  Nah too difficult to film.  But I have to get him past the camera somehow!  Ah!  He can knock the camera out of the way.   That took hours of filming to get the camera *back* into the correct spot.

Lin suggested I cut it but I was like "no way!" after I spent so long filming it.

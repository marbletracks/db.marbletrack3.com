## Status
In progress — being built from interviews with Rob, 2026-08-14 onward. Rob's own

## The outermost circle

The huge project is **not** Marble Track 3. MT3 is one expression of it.

The real subject is two lifelong interests meeting:

1. **Building materials** — LEGO and other construction systems.
2. **Time as a medium.** In Rob's words: *"my love of time as a medium in which we
   can play."*

Stop-motion is where those two meet: a building project whose material is wood and
glue, filmed in a medium whose material is time itself.

## Where the attraction started

Rob was fascinated as a kid by rolling-ball tracks — built with everything from
**bowling balls** to **billiard balls** to **1.25-inch steel balls**.

He went smaller than those, and the reason is physical, not aesthetic:

> "Because I can see well very close up, I've been attracted to creating smaller
> projects than these large billiard or bowling ball sized projects."

So the scale of MT3 — popsicle sticks, toothpicks, marbles — is a direct
consequence of how Rob's eyes work. That is worth saying out loud somewhere public;
it explains the whole project's texture.

## The three failed attempts (2011 – 2013)

Before the MT3 that exists, there were three that didn't.

- **2011, first attempt.** Rob built a tripod for his waterproof camera, waited for
  glue to dry, put down a piece of wood, shot some frames. This is the one Pinky
  was present for.
- **Second attempt.** *"There was apparently a second attempt but I have no
  biological memory of it."*
- **2013-05, third attempt.** Survives as a video labelled "Third attempt"
  (`https://youtu.be/kSdHNDlQ1x8`) — *"I still only made a minute and 9 seconds of
  video, but it was a great start!"* Rabby moved the stage into place in this one,
  which makes him older than the current film.

### Why they died

Two causes, and the second one is the best disaster story in the project.

**1. He couldn't reach the back.**

> "The first three versions of Marble Track 3 were too difficult to work with
> because it was fixed on the table and I couldn't easily reach to the back without
> knocking something over."

**2. He encrypted the backup and lost the password.**

> "On one of the above attempts, I had to give up because the frames of video were
> inaccessible after I encrypted the backup and subsequently lost the password. I
> was so afraid someone would steal the hard work! I was sure I was smart enough to
> keep a password safe."

**The backup was online**, not a local drive — so the frames are, presumably, still
sitting on somebody's servers, encrypted, indefinitely. Sealed permanently by their
own author out of fear that someone might steal them. Nobody stole them. Nobody can
look at them either.

**Ordering note:** the bearing was bought in **early 2017** and the house in
**summer 2017**, which puts the purchase *before* the move — while Rob's written
account tells it the other way round ("after Lin and I had bought a house... I went
to a home centre"). Both dates are his; the sequence is unpinned. Posts should avoid
claiming which came first.

### Why 2017 worked

> "Finally, in summer of 2017, after Lin and I had bought a house, and no plans to
> move! I decided to start the project again... Now that we had a house, I was more
> confident I could settle in for a big project. Soon enough I had blown past however
> many frames I had lost before."

**Stability was the precondition.** A stop-motion set cannot be moved — everything
must stay exactly where it is between sessions. Owning a house with no plans to move
is what made a nine-year set possible. The fourth attempt succeeded because the
furniture stopped moving.

## The lineage

### Before MT1: LEGO

The first attempts were LEGO, and they failed for reasons that determined everything
after: LEGO is *too opaque*, and the roof tiles are *too sloped* to make a satisfying
marble run. Softwood solved both.

### Marble Track 1 (c. 1983–84 → early 1990s)

It had no number. In Rob's words:

> "Marble Track 1 was 'my marble track' while I was building it. There was no
> numerical designation because it was the only one in existence."

The numbering is retroactive — MT1 only became MT1 when MT2 existed.

- Basic shape settled in **approximately 7th grade, age 13 or 14, 1983 or 1984**:
  popsicle sticks mounted on a square wooden slab.
- Built *"haphazardly over time"* until a couple of years after high school
  graduation.
- His friend **Maggie**, from the University of Houston, loved the track and
  encouraged him; he worked on it occasionally during university, but not much.
- **2003:** Rob left the United States for Japan and asked Maggie to be the
  caretaker of MT1 *"for a year or two while I was gone."*

### Marble Track 2 (finished c. 2008)

- Built after Rob had been away about five years, **intended from the start as a
  gift for Maggie**.
- The MT2 video's upload date marks when it was finished.
- Rob built a **custom box** for it and carried it by hand to the United States. It
  arrived intact at her house in Houston.
- He gave her MT2 — and at the same time officially *"gave"* her MT1, which had
  been in her possession for years already.
- **Denser than MT1**: same materials, but MT1 has far fewer tracks per volume.
  MT3's density can't be compared yet because it isn't finished.

> "I think there exists on YouTube a zoom video call that features both Marble
> Tracks 1 and 2 on her side and Marble Track 3 on mine."

*(Worth finding — that video has all three tracks in one frame.)*

### Materials, all three

Softwood: **popsicle sticks, toothpicks, wooden spoons, and slow-drying glue.**

### The pattern nobody has named yet

MT1 and MT2 both live in Houston, given away. `marbletrack3.com/content/support.md`
promises that *"the person or entity who has given the most support will receive the
actual Marble Track 3 track itself."* Rob gives his marble tracks away. Three for
three, if that promise stands.

## The origin of the conceit

The About page's gag — YouTube commenters saying MT2 was a waste of time — is real,
and the path from those comments to a stop-motion animation was not instant.

**The mean ones:**

> "It was a bit sad to read the mean YouTube comments, but I also could laugh at
> them because I literally had a girlfriend at the time. She was attracted to me
> partially because I made Marble Track 2."

**The supportive ones, which were the actually confusing ones:**

> "The supportive comments seemed nonsensical.. 'of course it's obvious how to make
> a marble track!!', but then I realized maybe it's not obvious... and that
> realization slowly morphed into the concept of making an animation of how to make
> a marble track."

So MT3 exists because strangers asked how, and Rob's first reaction — *it's obvious*
— turned out to be wrong. The project is an answer to a question he initially
thought didn't need answering.

**The rejected version:**

> "At first I thought of doing a time lapse of me creating it, but I knew that would
> be far too janky to do over a period of months. Then I conceived of having the
> characters build it."

The Workers are not decoration. They are the **solution to a technical problem** —
a months-long time-lapse of a man gluing sticks would have looked terrible, so the
builders became characters instead.

> "I probably would not have started it if I had known that it would take over a
> decade."

## The stop-motion problem

MT3 is **Rob's first stop-motion animation**. He notes the absurdity himself: it is
his first, and it is this enormous.

It is also **at least the second or third attempt to start MT3** since the MT2 video
was posted. (The DB and the archived worker descriptions put one earlier attempt at
**2011** — Pinky is described as having been present for it.)

> "The enormous challenge of making the stop-motion animation has made it difficult
> to actually finish marble track 3, but feedback I've gotten from people is so full
> of fascination that I feel inspired to do it."

This is the engine of the whole thing and the honest answer to "why isn't it
finished": the animation is what makes it hard, and other people's fascination is
what keeps it going. Note that this is the same shape as the About page's origin
gag — YouTube commenters saying it was a waste of time, and other commenters asking
how to make one.

When Rob started MT3 he knew it deserved its own project, but *"had no idea that it
would take so long to build."*

## The naming problem (unresolved)

Rob wants the mythology of **Marble Tracks 1 through 4** included — but that lineage
does not fit grammatically inside the name "Marble Track 3."

Proposed handling (not yet decided): leave the Patreon campaign as
`patreon.com/marbletrack3` and give the lineage its own **Collection** inside it, so
the umbrella needs a Collection title rather than a rename of anything.

# Concept and Vision

## Status
In progress — interviews with Rob, 2026-08-14 onward. Quotes are his exact words.

---

## Time as the medium

The project's thesis, in Rob's words:

> "The end result is that people get a movie and they don't care how long it took.
> But the time taken to make it look good is what makes it valuable, so they
> unconsciously do care how long it took. They just don't know it."

This is the whole argument for MT3 and, not incidentally, the whole argument for
supporting it. The viewer cannot see the nine years. But they can feel them — the
quality they're responding to *is* the time, arriving in a form they can't name.

### The Matrix comparison (Rob's own)

> "Keanu Reeves and all them studied martial arts for months in order to make a
> 2-hour Matrix movie look incredible because it really is the actors fighting. So
> during those months he (and I) have to keep the big vision in mind to keep doing
> the hard thing so the payoff is incredible."

Two things are packed in here, and both matter:

1. **Authenticity of effort.** It looks incredible *because it really is the actors
   fighting* — not because it was faked well. MT3's equivalent: the track really is
   being built, the Workers really are moved by hand, one frame at a time. Nothing
   is simulated.
2. **Holding the vision is the job.** The hard part is not the skill, it's keeping
   the payoff in mind for months so you keep doing the hard thing. That is the
   actual daily labour of MT3, and it's the part nobody sees.

### What it feels like from the inside

> "The experience of time just melts away until my brain locks up after a couple
> hours of overdrive and I have to take a break."

Flow, then a hard stop. Not fatigue that builds gradually — a lock-up. This matches
the episode posts, where two hours of livestream routinely yields a single-digit
number of frames:

> "Today in over 2 hours of live stream I took ~~about maybe four or five frames
> I'm guessing~~. (only three!!)"
> — `marbletrack3.com/content/episode/2020/12/15preparing-for-g-choppys-flight.md`

Three frames is a quarter of a second of movie. That ratio is the project.

## What keeps him going: the laughter

Asked what vision he holds at the rig to keep placing frames, Rob's answer was not
the finished movie, the glass case, or the museum. It was the joke landing:

> "The thing that keeps me going is the laughter, omg it's so hilarious and makes
> perfect sense that G Choppy would do that, or that Squarehead would drop that."

Note *"makes perfect sense that"* — the comedy is **character-driven and
consistency-driven**. It's funny because it's exactly what that Worker would do.
That is why `docs/worker_voices/` matters beyond the website: the voice profiles
are the machinery the humour runs on. A Worker who behaves inconsistently isn't
just off-model, he isn't funny.

He also volunteered a note against his own instincts:

> "It's okay to take lots of frames; overall the whole movie is *far too fast!*"

## What breaks the brain: N timescales at once

The lock-up Rob described earlier has a specific cause — holding several
incompatible clocks in mind simultaneously:

> "The thing that breaks my brain is having to think in N different timescales at
> once. E.g. 'How long has G Choppy been holding his arm up? It seems like a long
> time..' (e.g. it could be an hour or a few months of stasis)."

A Worker's raised arm is a fraction of a second on screen, an hour of Rob's
afternoon, and possibly *months* of real elapsed time if the shot spans a pause in
production. All three are true at once, and only one of them is visible.

### "The frames never lie"

Rob's phrase, and the discipline the whole thing runs on. The corollary is brutal:

> "Oh crap, speaking of frames not lying, I forgot to move Pinky! Do I delete these
> four frames which erase the entirety of what I filmed today?"

A continuity error is not fixable in the edit — it is only deletable. An afternoon's
work can evaporate because one character stood still while the world moved.

### The other brain-breaker: balance

> "Also brain breaking are when characters won't balance where I want them and the
> video looks all janky, or requires me to double-shoot frames with and without
> character and merge in post production."

Pipe-cleaner figures do not reliably stand where physics is asked to put them. The
fallback — shoot the frame twice, with and without the figure, composite afterwards
— means some finished shots contain a Worker who was never actually standing there.

## There is no second take

The movie's pace is not a choice that can be revisited. It is a permanent property
of the object:

> "The whole movie moves too fast and it's baked in because I cannot reshoot things
> once the glue has been glued. The only way to reshoot would be to literally
> recreate the entire thing, which is also impossible because pieces have been
> custom cut including breaking along organic lines, and glued at organic angles."

This is the hardest fact in the whole project and probably its best story. The track
is not reproducible — not because it would be expensive, but because the pieces
broke the way they broke. A snapped popsicle stick has an organic fracture line that
cannot be cut again. Every shot is a single take of an unrepeatable object.

So: the movie is too fast, forever, and the only alternative was not existing.

## The ending: the museum party

The finish line is an event, not an artifact.

> "Inviting patrons to a museum party would signal the end of the project. The
> museum party would be the actual end of Marble Track 3 and the beginning of
> Marble Track 4 inauguration."

Three things follow:

1. **The museum room is real and is the ending** — not a daydream in the README.
2. **Patrons are the guest list.** That is a materially better perk than any
   digital bonus, and it costs nothing to promise now.
3. **MT4 begins where MT3 ends**, at the same party. The lineage isn't retrospective
   mythology; it's a running series with a scheduled handover.

This gives the Patreon milestone series an ending to point at. Every backdated post
is a step toward a party the reader could actually be invited to.

## Why this matters for Patreon

The support pitch follows directly from the thesis and does not need to beg:

- The public gets the movie and doesn't see the clock.
- **Patrons are the people who know what it cost.** Supporting isn't charity for a
  man gluing toothpicks; it's paying attention to the invisible thing that makes the
  visible thing good.

That framing also makes the *drip* of milestone posts coherent — each one is a
receipt for time already spent.
